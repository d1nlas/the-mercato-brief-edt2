01 / OPENING NOTE

Miss this edition, and how will you know what everyone’s talking about?

FOMO works because being left out rarely feels like a neutral state. Someone else is in the room, in the conversation, at the drop, and suddenly absence feels like something worth fixing.

In an age of information overload, attention is scarce. FOMO steps into that scarcity as a psychological lever, turning a vague anxiety about absence into a reason to act now.

The most effective campaigns do not merely sell an object. They sell the reassurance of not being left out.

03 / THE PLAYBOOK

Five ways brands
manufacture urgency.

A content analysis of 1,372 social media posts identified five recurring strategic categories.

01

Time-limited offers

“Ends tonight.” “Last 3 hours.” A prospective loss frame turns time into pressure, and pressure into conversion.

WHY IT WORKS

Time pressure reframes waiting as a potential loss, shortening the gap between consideration and action.

02

Scarcity cues

“Only 2 left.” “Low stock.” Limited availability can amplify perceived value, symbolic meaning and engagement.

WHY IT WORKS

Limited availability raises the perceived stakes: if the product may disappear, hesitation suddenly feels expensive.

03

Social proof

“327 people are viewing this.” Participation becomes visible, and suddenly the consumer is comparing their absence.

WHY IT WORKS

Visible participation turns a private decision into a social comparison. Other people become evidence that something is happening now.

04

Peer-pressure cues

Messages from the social circle often carry more weight than brand-driven communication. The messenger changes the pressure.

WHY IT WORKS

When the cue comes from people we know or identify with, the pressure feels socially relevant rather than merely promotional.

05

Exclusive access

Members-only perks, invite-only systems and early access create an in-group, and make everyone outside it want in.

WHY IT WORKS

Exclusivity creates an in-group. The desire is no longer only for the product, but for the status of being inside.

FOMO STACK

One cue can create urgency. Several cues together can create a whole behavioural environment.

04 / THE CASES

Four brands.
Four kinds of fear.

Across categories, the strongest work does not simply shout “act now.” It gives the fear a story, a face, or a world the consumer does not want to miss.

01

FROM FOMO TO FOMSI

Yahoo turned a vague unease into a named problem.

In its campaign featuring Cardi B, Yahoo built around FOMSI: Fear of Missing Something Important. The story begins with the anxiety of overlooking something critical, then introduces Yahoo Mail Planner, which uses AI to extract schedules and to-dos from emails.

THE MOVEName the anxiety; then sell the cure.
02

ABANDONED NIGHTS

AB InBev made the missed purchase feel like a missed night.

For TaDa, abandoned carts triggered personalised AI videos predicting how epic the user's night could have been. Names, neighbourhoods and basket items became the raw material for humorous stories, distributed through email, SMS and WhatsApp.

THE MOVETurn a negative behaviour into a re-engaging experience.
03

THE BIG MISS OUT

AlUla made attendance feel like cultural belonging.

The AlUla Tour 2026 campaign framed the moment around a simple tension: people may not care deeply about a specific race or sport, but they care when everyone else seems to be experiencing something exciting, culturally resonant and important. The campaign generated 179 million interactions.

THE MOVEMake the event feel bigger than the event.
04

STRAW CHARMS / COLLECTOR CULTURE

Starbucks turned a purchase into participation.

Starbucks Philippines paired limited-edition beverage charms with curiosity, FOMO and repeat visits. Research around the Starbucks Bear Cup phenomenon shows how social media showcases, community engagement and symbolic participation can deepen product meaning.

THE MOVEScarcity becomes stronger when people can show they got in.
05 / INDIA

FOMO, Indian edition.

Here, the feeling often arrives through a screen that disappears, a package next door, or a countdown that refuses to let you linger.

01

DISAPPEARING CONTENT

24 hours is enough time to make “later” feel like “never.”

Brands including Swiggy, Nykaa and Myntra are using Instagram and Facebook Stories to create urgency around key digital campaigns because the content disappears after 24 hours.

02

FASHION ANXIETY

Myntra sold the feeling of being left behind.

“It’s You 2.0” captures delivery envy: a mother watches her neighbour receive endless Myntra packages while her own budget imposes a boundary.

03

LIVE SCARCITY

“Only 100 left” makes the shelf feel smaller.

Zomato and Swiggy Instamart use low-stock alerts to manufacture urgency, while Myntra’s Early Access sales turn exclusivity into in-app engagement.

JOMO
THE COUNTER-MOVEMENT

Sometimes the smartest consumer is the one who is comfortable missing out.

A segment of Indian consumers is gradually shifting from FOMO to the Joy of Missing Out. In a noisy market, calm confidence can build stronger long-term loyalty than permanent scarcity.

06 / A MERCATO BRIEF TAKEAWAY

The fear gets the click.
The brand has to earn what comes next.

FOMO can create the first move, but it cannot carry the whole relationship. The strongest marketers know when to turn urgency down and let relevance, trust and meaning take over.

01

Start with psychology

Understand what the audience is afraid of losing, not just what the campaign wants them to buy.

02

Protect the trust

Urgency works until people stop believing the signal. Then the tactic becomes the problem.

03

Use technology with intent

AI can scale personalised FOMO, but scale only matters when the story still feels human.

04

Read the local room

India is building its own language of missing out, shaped by culture, platforms and everyday behaviour.

THE QUESTION TO LEAVE WITH

Maybe the real question is not whether people are afraid to miss out.

What will make them want to stay?
